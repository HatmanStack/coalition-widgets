Coalition to End Homelessness · Wichita & Sedgwick County

State of Homelessness

The cost of the basics has outrun what working families earn, and more households are losing housing than at any point in six years. This is what that looks like in Sedgwick County, and what the Coalition's 25 member agencies do about it.

2025 · Coalition partners

5,082

people were served by Coalition partners last year, across 84,079 individual services

3,358people housed or exited homelessness
1,360exited to positive housing outcomes
565people in priority populations reviewed at weekly case conferencing

Twenty-five member agencies work a single coordinated pathway: outreach, shelter, wraparound services, permanent housing, then ongoing support. Case conferencing is a weekly multi-agency meeting that works individual barriers to housing, person by person.

Source: 2026 State of Homelessness Report, 2025 outcomes.

How well the system works

Two things the Coalition controls directly: how long someone stays homeless once they enter the system, and whether housing holds afterward.

Days spent homeless, on average. The national figure is for 2024, from HUD's Longitudinal Systems Analysis. Shorter is the whole point: every day someone spends homeless is a day of compounding harm and a day a shelter bed is unavailable to somebody else.

Two years after support ends

Housing holds

82% of people housed are still housedtwo years on

Rehousing that does not hold is not an outcome, it is a delay. Measuring retention two years after support ends is a harder test than counting placements, and the Coalition publishes it.

Recognized for data quality in 2026 Community Solutions confirmed the Coalition can identify at least 90% of single adults experiencing homelessness in Sedgwick County by name and by need. Knowing who is out there is the first step to ending their homelessness, and most communities cannot do it.

Source: 2026 State of Homelessness Report.

Moving people out, faster than they arrive

The Coalition reaches functional zero when more people leave homelessness in a year than enter it. Veterans are already there. Countywide, the gap is closing against a rising tide.

2025 · everyone

Countywide

3,358 people left homelessness last year. Inflow ran 219 ahead. The gap is not a throughput problem. Households becoming homeless for the first time are up 147% since 2020, driven by housing costs the Coalition does not set. Exits include temporary destinations such as family, friends, hospitals and jail.

Source: 2026 State of Homelessness Report, inflow and outflow, 2025.

Three quarters of the people counted were in shelter or transitional housing rather than outside, which is what a functioning shelter system looks like on a freezing night. More than 120 volunteers canvassed the county to survey people sleeping outside. Colder weather pushes more people into shelter on count night, and better method and volunteer training also move the number, so year-over-year change reflects both real conditions and better counting.

For scale: in 2025 the local rate was 14 people per 10,000 residents, against a national rate of 23 per 10,000 in 2024.

How to read these numbers

Point-in-Time countA one-night census taken each January, required by HUD. It measures conditions on that night, not how well the response is working. Always smaller than the number of people served over a period.
People servedEveryone who received shelter, outreach, or housing services from a Coalition partner during the year.
Inflow and outflowHow many people became homeless in a year against how many were housed or exited. Functional zero is reached when outflow exceeds inflow.
Actively homelessSomeone with a live HMIS enrollment on the last day of the period who has no move-in date and has not exited to permanent housing.
HouseholdOne or more people living together who intend to stay together once housed. A family household includes children; a youth household's oldest member is 18 to 24.
Cost burdenedSpending 30% or more of household income on housing. Severely burdened is 50% or more.
ALICEAsset Limited, Income Constrained, Employed. Households earning above the federal poverty level but below the cost of necessities.
HMISHomeless Management Information System, the shared database Coalition partners record into.
Programs countedEmergency shelter, coordinated entry, permanent housing, rapid rehousing, street outreach and transitional housing.
